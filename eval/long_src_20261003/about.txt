Frozen evaluation set for quotations inside realistic LONG Arabic articles, v1 (2026-10-03), written for the planned rise of the article length limit from 6,000 to 20,000 characters. AUTHOR-WRITTEN BY AI AND NOT HUMAN-REVIEWED: the articles and every label were written on 3 Oct 2026 by an AI subagent (Claude) working in a fresh context, in one session (the data were corrected against the validator's label contract until it passed; a few prose passages were also reworded after the validator listed unlabelled Quran-like runs in them, which is not tuning to any detector); the prose is hand-written by that subagent (eval/long_src_20261003/a01_*.py ... a10_*.py), the Quran words are cut from the Quranpedia Hafs text by coordinates (eval/long_src_20261003/engine.py; no bulk Quran corpus file is stored), and the labels were derived from the Quranpedia text and from the contract of eval/validate_articles_long_20261003.py ONLY, NOT from the behaviour of the detector (the author did not run the app's audit on these articles, did not start the server, and did not read the detection code). It is therefore not an independent sample: the same assistant wrote the texts and the labels, so the articles probably favour wordings it finds natural, and the statistics describe agreement with this author's labelling, not accuracy on real writers. NO THRESHOLD, RULE, PROMPT OR LIMIT MAY BE TUNED AGAINST THIS FILE; it is frozen by checksum (SHA-256 in eval/articles_long_20261003.sha256), and a rerun after any change to the app must disclose that the set is no longer held out once anything has been tuned on it. Every label is cross-checked by eval/validate_articles_long_20261003.py, which does not use the app's matching code. Contents: 10 articles of 8,060-14,700 characters (102,356 in total; three over 14,000), 191 gold quotations from 65 different surahs, 45 negatives and 33 formulas. Topics: knowledge, trade ethics, technology and the heart, patience, family, environment, time (a Friday sermon), charity (numbered footnotes), neighbours (readers' letters), youth (a graduation speech). Each article has real paragraph structure (headings, lists, a few footnote-style references, a Latin-script word or URL here and there). Quotations are marked (with the Quran brackets or guillemets) or unmarked inside the prose, in plain, vocalized or Uthmani spelling, complete or partial, single-verse or multi-verse, short or long; 68 of the 191 carry a wording error (a missing, an extra or a wrong word, a wrong first or last word, or two edits), the rest are exact; the written reference is correct, wrong (a valid but different place), out of range (an ayah number beyond the surah), surah-only or missing, in short form, long form (surah name, ayah), with Arabic-Indic digits, before the quotation, after it, or in a footnote paragraph. A few ayahs are quoted twice in one article (repeated), and a few quotations are phrases that occur in several places of the Quran (ambiguous = true). The gold ayahs avoid the gold ayahs of eval/articles_frozen.json. negatives are hadith, proverbs and sentences of the author that are not Quran text; formulas are common expressions that are Quran text. Same fields as eval/articles_frozen.json (id, category, article, gold, negatives, formulas; gold: quote, surah, ayah_start, ayah_end, correct_text, wording = correct | wording_error, ambiguous, reference_text, reference = correct | incorrect | out_of_range | surah_only | missing, unmarked, tags); tags are derived from the text by the builder and re-derived by the validator (marked/unmarked, plain/vocalized/uthmani, single_verse/multi_verse, full_verse/partial/incomplete, short, wording_error, missing_word, extra_word, one_word_wrong, wrong_first_word, wrong_last_word, numeric_ref, range_ref, long_form_ref, arabic_indic_digits, wrong_reference, no_reference, repeated_in_quran) plus the position tags inline_ref / ref_before / footnote_ref and repeated, which the validator checks against where the reference text sits. The validator also lists, for information, every run of three or more article words that happens to occur in the Quran outside the gold quotations (inside a negative, a formula, or plain prose): such runs are common words of Arabic prose and religious style and are not labelled; they are reported, not removed.
